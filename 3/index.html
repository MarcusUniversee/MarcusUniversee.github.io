Project 3: [Auto]Stitching Photo Mosaics

In this project, we stich together photographs to create larger composite images.

Part A: Image Warping and Mosaicing

Part A.1: Shoot the Pictures

First, we need to shoot the pictures to create the mosaics! An important part is to keep our center of projection the same, since single homographies require this, so I took all photos while only rotating my cell phone.

Below are images that I took!

Inside of Berkeley's East Asian Library, rotating the camera along the z (vertical) axis. (left most to right most)

Part A.2: Recover Homographies

In this section, we use linear algebra to compute homographies from correspondences

Large Left → Middle Homography

$$\begin{bmatrix} 4.648203 & 0.057244 & -4.076861\times 10^{3} \\ 1.363090 & 3.956188 & -1.807942\times 10^{3} \\ 0.002494 & 7.496353\times 10^{-5} & 1.000000 \end{bmatrix}$$

Slight Left → Middle Homography

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1.646451 & -0.070159 & -738.010122 \\ 0.240919 & 1.263833 & -188.362915 \\ 5.069110\times 10^{-4} & -1.444826\times 10^{-4} & 1.000000 \end{bmatrix}$$

Slight Right → Middle Homography

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0.526235 & -0.071124 & 485.834174 \\ -0.125217 & 0.752835 & 94.401809 \\ -3.011633\times 10^{-4} & -5.149075\times 10^{-5} & 1.000000 \end{bmatrix}$$

Large Right → Middle Homography

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0.131936 & 0.003065 & 882.366432 \\ -0.291282 & 0.841679 & 69.541333 \\ -6.188067\times 10^{-4} & 3.779466\times 10^{-5} & 1.000000 \end{bmatrix}$$

Part A.3: Warp the Images

In this section, we warp the images in two ways: Nearest Neighbor and Bilinear Interpolation using the homography matrix

The original large left and middle images

The left image "warped" into the plane of the middle image. (left) is nearest neighbor, which looks noisier and has more artifacts. (right) is bilinear, which looks cleaner but has a dark outline.
