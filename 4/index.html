Part 1: Fit a Neural Field to a 2D Image

In this section, we fit a neural field to two images. I used the same MLP neural network from the spec (architecture shown below). This neural network has four linear layers of width 256, 256, 256, and 3. Each layer is followed by a RELU activation function except for the fourth layer, which is followed by a Sigmoid activation function to constrain output from 0 to 1. Before our inputs are passed into a model, we positionally encode the points at a max frequency of L = 10, leading to 2 (original x, y) + 10 * 2 (sin and cosine) * 2 (x, y). I used the positional encoding equation given in the spec. The overall input is pixel coordinates to RGB.

Here are the training results on the given test image and my own image at steps: 0, 50, 120, 200, 500, final (3000), and then the original image

I then vary the parameters width and L, setting width to 64 (so the linear layers are width 64, 64, 64, 3) and L to 4. Here are the results for all four combinations. These results are very interesting! It seems that decreasing max frequency (L) makes the image more smooth and less detailed, while decreasing width adds noise to the image.

Top left: L=10, width=256. Top right: L=10, width=64
Top left: L=4, width=256. Top right: L=4, width=64

To evaluate reconstruction quality of an image, I used PSNR (Peak signal-to-noise). Here is ratio curve for the stuffed animal image with L=10 and width=256. After around the 150th iteration, the PSNR plateau'd and only had smaller improvements.
